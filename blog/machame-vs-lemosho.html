Machame vs Lemosho: Which Kilimanjaro Route Fits You

May 9, 20268 min readKilimanjaro

These two routes account for the majority of Kilimanjaro climbs we book, and guests almost always ask us to just pick one. Here's the actual case for each.

The numbers first

MachameLemosho
Typical duration6–7 days8–9 days
Summit success rate~82–87%~90–95%
Starting cost (from)$2,150$2,650
CrowdingModerate to busy — the most popular route on the mountainLighter, especially the first two days through Shira
Scenery varietyStrong — the "Whiskey Route" nickname reflects both its popularity and its terrain varietyBest on the mountain — approaches through untouched rainforest most other routes don't use

Why Machame is booked more often

Machame hits a practical sweet spot: it's shorter than Lemosho (meaning lower total cost even though the per-day rate is similar), it's well-established with predictable camps and trail conditions, and its acclimatization profile — the "climb high, sleep low" pattern through the Lava Tower — is genuinely effective for a 6–7 day itinerary. The trade-off is that it's the most heavily used route on the mountain, so camps can feel crowded in peak season (July–October, and around the New Year window).

Why we recommend Lemosho when budget allows

The extra one to two days aren't padding — they're the actual mechanism behind Lemosho's higher success rate. More time at altitude before the summit push measurably reduces altitude-sickness dropout rates, which is the single biggest reason climbers don't reach Uhuru Peak (fitness is rarely the limiting factor). Lemosho also approaches from the west through genuinely quiet rainforest, crosses the Shira Plateau, and only merges with the Machame route around Barranco Camp — so you get two to three days of noticeably less crowded trail before joining the busier southern circuit.

If your budget and schedule can stretch to it, we default to recommending Lemosho for most first-time climbers — not because Machame is a worse route, but because the extra acclimatization day is the highest-leverage decision you can make before the climb even starts.

When Machame is the better call anyway

  • You have firm time constraints and can't add the extra 1–2 days
  • You've already done a high-altitude trek in the past 12 months and acclimatize quickly
  • Budget is a hard limit — Machame's shorter itinerary meaningfully reduces total cost (fewer porter-days, less food, fewer camping fees)
  • You want the option to combine with more safari or beach days in a fixed total trip length

What both routes get right

Neither route requires technical climbing experience. Both pass through Kilimanjaro's genuinely remarkable five climate zones — rainforest, moorland, alpine desert, and the arctic summit zone — in a single trip, which is part of what makes this mountain unusual among non-technical climbs. And on both, your realistic odds come down far more to pacing and hydration discipline than to raw fitness.

The question we ask every guest isn't "which route sounds more scenic" — it's "how many days do you actually have, and how much does that extra night at altitude matter to you."

If you're still unsure, tell Mihayo AI (the assistant on our homepage) or our team your travel dates and fitness background — route selection is genuinely one of the few decisions on this mountain worth spending real time on before you commit.
